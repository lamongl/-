# Blind comparison of A and B

## Verdict

B is the better examination presentation, by a modest but practically useful margin. Both meet the central deliverable requirements and closely follow the paper. B improves how an examiner can inspect the argument: its research question includes learning and China, its central argument appears on slide 2, its source-to-claim boundary table appears before the substantive claims, and its reserve questions anticipate more challenging objections. This is not a large transformation in presentation quality: the visual systems and underlying arguments are very similar, and both would benefit from a concrete worked engineering example.

The observed difference is meaningful for defence readiness, especially evidence boundaries and Q&A, but does not by itself establish that either production method is generally superior. No numeric quality score is warranted.

## Review scope and evidence

I reviewed only the anonymous A/B PPTX packages, their extracted text/notes JSON, all sixteen supplied slide PNGs, and the original DOCX paper. I did not inspect the production arms, assignments, receipts, builders, prior decks, or Skill instructions. A/B identity remains unknown to this reviewer.

Package inspection confirms eight slide XML parts and eight notes parts in each deck. A contains 59 native shape elements and no slide pictures; B contains 50 native shape elements, two native tables, and no slide pictures. This supports editability rather than a deck of flattened screenshots. It does not establish that every editing interaction works in native PowerPoint. The PNGs are existing render evidence; I did not conduct a native PowerPoint presentation or notes-view check.

## Fidelity, density and graphic usefulness

Neither deck introduces a serious contradiction of the supplied paper. Both retain the three linked claims, student perspective, engineering foundations, tentative role shift, risk-sensitive review and limits on legal conclusions. Both correctly avoid treating policy intent as observed adoption or foreign survey results as China-wide measurement. Future empirical studies are explicitly described as proposed extensions. Both omit the industry revenue statistic and most cognitive-bias percentages; that is sensible prioritisation for five minutes, rather than loss of a central result.

B's claim to be a literature/policy synthesis is a defensible description of this discussion paper, not evidence of a systematic review. Neither deck claims a reproducible search method or new experiment. B's Q&A answer about biased actions correctly avoids a causal claim. A's exact Finnish sample figures agree with the paper. I found no serious factual distortion in the decks relative to the DOCX.

Both have large, readable typography, restrained colour, consistent alignment and generous margins. All sixteen supplied renders show complete pages without obvious clipped content. A relies almost entirely on paired text rows; B adds two useful tables. The advantage is functional grouping, not decoration. Neither has an actual process diagram, code example, or concrete failure-and-review case. A small example showing an AI draft, a failed requirement/security check and a human release decision would make the argument more memorable, but its absence is not a compliance failure. Neither needs charts of quantities that the paper did not measure.

Both remain somewhat repetitive: coordination, review, judgment and responsibility recur on multiple slides. B's explicit argument on slide 2 and the evidence-first ordering help the audience understand why those repetitions belong together. A follows the paper's role-learning-responsibility sequence more directly, which is also coherent.

## Limits and recommended interpretation

There is no external reference verification in this review. Both decks openly state in their notes that references are taken from the paper and not independently checked. Accordingly, source authenticity, DOI validity, and the policy claims remain unverified; the finding here is fidelity to the supplied paper, not independent validation of its bibliography. Neither deck displays full author-year citations on the projected slides, so an audience seeing only the slides has limited traceability, even though the presenter notes supply sources. This is a shared academic usability limitation, not evidence of fabricated content.

My preference is B for an academic defence, with A close behind. B's advantage comes from a clearer early thesis, a genuinely useful evidence-boundary table and stronger objection handling. It does not come from extra words, greater novelty, or a more elaborate visual theme. A remains a credible presentation and is slightly stronger in separately explaining Agent development and precisely bounding what verification tools check. No major content or rendering defect disqualifies either deck.
